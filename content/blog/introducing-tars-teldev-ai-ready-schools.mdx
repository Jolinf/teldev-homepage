Watch a good coach at work and you notice something. They don't start by changing everything. They start by watching.

A sprinter might be losing a fraction of a second at the start. A footballer might be strong on one foot and hesitant on the other. A good coach spots it, gives the athlete something better to do today, and then builds training around exactly what they saw. Nobody has to be failing to deserve a coach. There is always more to gain, and someone who knows where to look can find it.

We think every classroom deserves the same.

## A student you have met

Think of a student in SS2. She is bright, she works hard, and she is quietly struggling with algebra. Every new topic seems to assume something she doesn't have. What she doesn't know is that the gap didn't start this term. It started in JSS1, with fractions, on a day she was too shy to say *"I don't get it"* and the class moved on.

Since then, she has learned to memorise enough to pass. She moves up a class every year. Her report card looks fine. And the gap underneath grows a little wider.

She isn't one real student. But if you have taught a class, sat in one or raised a child, you have met her. And she is far from alone: [World Bank analysis](https://businessday.ng/news/article/nigerias-37m-children-cant-read-understand-simple-text-world-bank/) suggests about 4 in 10 students reach the end of junior secondary school without foundational numeracy.

Her exam results will eventually show that she is struggling. They will never show *why*. A missing concept from years ago? A question she misread? A class of sixty where nobody noticed? Practice that never came? Each answer needs a different kind of help, and without knowing which, any help is a guess.

## What a good coach would do

That is the thinking behind the TARS initiative, the **TELDEV AI-Ready Schools Initiative**. It works the way a good coach does.

**First, something better to do today.** AI is already in students' hands. Used well, it is a patient tutor that never runs out of explanations. Used badly, it does the homework and teaches nothing. So we start with workshops that show students how to use AI to understand topics, practise and check their own work, and show teachers how to use it to save hours on preparation. The question our student was afraid to ask in JSS1, she can ask tonight, as many times as she needs.

It works. In a [World Bank pilot in Edo State](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/education/From-chalkboards-to-chatbots-Transforming-learning-in-Nigeria), secondary school students who used AI as a tutor, with teachers guiding them, gained the equivalent of nearly two years of typical learning in six weeks.

**Then, a closer look.** With the school's permission and parents' consent, we look carefully at how students learn, how teachers teach, and what gets in the way. A university research partner leads this work and signs off what it finds. For our student's school, it might show that half of SS2 is stuck on the same JSS1 concept, something no report card would ever reveal.

**Then, support built around what we find.** With partners, we strengthen teaching, materials and school conditions where the evidence shows the biggest gains, so the help reaches every student, including those with no phone or laptop at home.

**Finally, the right tools.** Only then do we build technology: a learning and teaching assistant that helps a student find her missing step, and helps her teacher see who needs help, and where.

Each step earns the next. We won't build a product before we understand the problem.

## The teacher is the head coach

In sport, analysts and specialists don't replace the head coach. They make the head coach better. The same is true here.

Teachers bring what no technology can: judgement, encouragement and real knowledge of the students in front of them. But a teacher with sixty students and a pile of paperwork can't give every student the attention they need, however good they are.

So teachers are an equal focus of TARS. We want to learn which teaching methods help students most, and how much of a teacher's week goes to administration, and then give that time back to teaching. This is support, not appraisal: nothing we gather is ever used to judge or rank a teacher.

## What we want to achieve

- **Students** who understand, not just pass, and who use AI to learn rather than to skip learning.
- **Teachers** with better tools, clearer information about their students, and more time to teach.
- **Schools** that know where their students can gain the most, and what will help them get there.
- And over time, **a generation better prepared** for the jobs, businesses and ideas Nigeria's future depends on.

## Where we are

We launched in October 2026. The workshops are written, and our first invitations have gone out to secondary schools across Lagos. No sessions have run yet. When they do, we will share what happened here, including what didn't work.
